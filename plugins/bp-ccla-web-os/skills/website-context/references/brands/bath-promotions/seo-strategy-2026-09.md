## The one finding that changes the strategy: real, measured cross-brand cannibalization
The existing docs treat "BP = distributor/wholesale angle, CCLA = direct-buyer angle" on shared terms (prayer candles, custom logo/branded candles) as a policy that keeps the two sites from competing. **Today's live Semrush data shows that policy is not holding at the SERP level.** On nearly every shared head term, Google currently ranks **customcandlesla.com** ahead of **bathpromotions.com** — often by a wide margin — regardless of which brand's page is "supposed" to own the term:

`customcandlesla.com` is also literally Semrush's own #1 organic "competitor" domain to `bathpromotions.com` by shared keyword overlap (0.23 relevance, 12 common keywords) — see file 04. This is not a hypothetical risk flagged in a schema; it is the measured current state. Domain-strength is part of the reason (CCLA: Semrush Rank 1,375,175, 723/mo traffic vs. BP: Rank 3,755,269, 104/mo traffic) — CCLA is simply the stronger domain today, so Google defaults to it on ambiguous, shared-topic queries.

**Recommendation (flagged for your decision, not yet acted on):** either (a) BP's prayer-candle and custom-logo-candle content needs a real authority push (dedicated pillar pages, not blog mentions) to compete with its own sister site, or (b) formally cede those specific head terms to CCLA and have BP concentrate exclusively on terms CCLA doesn't touch at all (private label soap/bath salts/spa products, hotel amenities, corporate gift candles) — several of which are already BP's strongest, least-contested opportunities (see file 07). Recommendation (b) is the lower-risk path given BP's current domain strength.
