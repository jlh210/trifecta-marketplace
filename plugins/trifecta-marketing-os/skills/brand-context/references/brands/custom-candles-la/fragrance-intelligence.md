# Custom Candles LA — Fragrance & Scent-Category Intelligence

**Why this file exists:** `ccla-blog-generator` (the skill that writes CCLA's SEO blog posts) assumes CCLA has an "8 fragrance family + color" system and refuses to fabricate one if it can't find real data — its own SKILL.md flags this as a structural placeholder, not real brand data. This file is the result of actually looking for it. What's below is real, sourced material — not invented — with the gap called out plainly where one still exists. Marketing and sales should both treat this as the current source of truth for scent naming and grouping.

## What's real: a 4-category scent organization system

Found in CCLA's own Google Drive, under "CCLA Fragrance Images" → "CCLA Fragrance Sample Bottles" — a folder actually used to organize product photography of fragrance sample bottles by category. This is the closest thing to a real "fragrance family" system that exists anywhere in the business right now:

**Natural Aromatics** — Australian Eucalyptus, Thai Lemongrass Citronella, French Lavender

**Bakery & Bar** — Moon Milk Latte, Champagne, Kentucky Bourbon Whiskey, Happy Birthday Cake, Vanilla Crème, Almond Amaretti Cookie, Chocolate Layer Cake

**Luxury Blends** — Cedarwood & Vetiver, Poolside, Hampton's Ocean Breeze, Sorrento Olive Lemon & Sage, Green Fig & Bergamot, Amber Santal, Sacred Nag Champa, Burnished Dark Leather, Fresh Cut Grass, Clean Cotton & Fresh Linen

**Fruits & Florals** — Smoked Rose, White Gardenia, Sugared Citrus, BAIES (Diptique-Type)

**Important correction to carry forward:** this is **4 real categories, not 8**. `ccla-blog-generator`'s assumption of an 8-family system does not match anything findable in CCLA's real materials as of this review. Treat "8 families" as an unconfirmed assumption in that skill, not a fact to fill in — if an 8-family version exists somewhere (e.g., with Angel or in a source not yet surfaced), it should replace this section; until then, 4 is the real, evidenced number.

## What's real: a separate list of production-ready scent names

Found in "CCLA - Fragrance Labels" — actual printed label PDFs, i.e. real, shipped product names, not concepts: Romantic White Gardenia, Laurel Canyon Lavender, Fresh Cut Stems, San Marino Berries & Rose, Grapefruit Spritzer, San Vicente Spa, Home Again Vanilla, Fancy Holiday Cookie, Beverly Hills Champagne, OC Citrus Blend, Catalina Sea Mist, DTLA Speakeasy, Leo Carillo Fire Pit, La Brea Leather, Palisades Fig & Bergamot, Patchouli Vetiver, Joshua Tree Santal, Malibu Sound Bath, Fruity Cereal, Cozy Vanilla, Bday Cake, Choco Chip.

This list uses LA-neighborhood-flavored naming (Laurel Canyon, San Vicente, DTLA, Palisades, Malibu, Joshua Tree, Catalina, Leo Carillo) that the sample-bottle category list above does not — likely a distinct, more polished naming layer applied at the point of production/labeling. **These two lists have not been reconciled against each other** — some scents plausibly describe the same fragrance under a plainer working name (sample-bottle side) and a branded label name (label side), e.g. "White Gardenia" / "Romantic White Gardenia," but this file does not assert that mapping since it wasn't found written down anywhere. Whoever owns label naming (likely Angel or Julie) can confirm true 1:1 matches if that's useful.

## What's real, but shared with Bath Promotions

Several individual scent names above (Champagne, Almond Amaretti Cookie, Australian Eucalyptus, Sacred Nag Champa, Clean Cotton & Fresh Linen, BAIES (Diptique-Type), French Lavender, Cedarwood & Vetiver) also appear in Bath Promotions' own "Fragrance Library.csv" (a flat, un-categorized ~90-scent list at `bathpromotions.com/collections/scents`). This is consistent with the Brand Operating System doc's note that both brands share one manufacturing origin — they likely draw from a shared underlying scent library, even though CCLA and Bath Promotions apply completely different visual/brand treatment on top of it (see CCLA's Visual Direction below vs. Bath Promotions' separate, industrial-toned profile).

## The color side — still a real gap

No document, spreadsheet, or folder anywhere in CCLA's real materials maps a color to a fragrance family or an individual scent. What *does* exist, and is safe to use:

- **CCLA's overall brand hex palette** (already documented above in `profile.md`): Deep Magenta `#981B45` (primary), Rose Pink `#CD4D78` (secondary), Dusty Rose `#C67C92` (accent), Warm Blush `#DFBCA6`, Muted Teal `#4F6D7A` (contrast), Deep Plum `#4C2B36` (dark anchor).
- **CCLA's visual/photography palette** (from the real Brand Operating System doc, distinct from the hex set above — this describes mood and material, not literal swatches): warm neutrals, deep blacks, amber gold, frosted white, moody blues — "rich, editorial, atmospheric."

Neither of these is broken down per fragrance family. If a blog post, product page, or campaign needs "the color for Bakery & Bar" or "the color for Fruits & Florals" specifically, that pairing does not exist yet and should not be invented — ask Angel/Julie, or treat it as a follow-up decision (a proposed draft pairing can be built on request, clearly labeled as a draft pending their sign-off).

## Where this should be used

Cross-departmental, not just content: marketing (blog posts, product descriptions, campaign theming) and sales (talking about the CCLA scent range accurately when qualifying hospitality/gifting/brand-visionary leads) should both treat the two scent lists above as current and real, and should not describe a color-per-fragrance system as if it exists.

`ccla-blog-generator`'s own `reference/` folder (referenced in its SKILL.md as holding `brand-intelligence-snapshot.md`) does not exist in this environment and should eventually be pointed at this file as its real source, the same way the ICP guide became the source of truth for `lead-generation-specialist`.
